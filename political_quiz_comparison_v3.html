Jack's Political Typology

I primarily think about politics in terms of values and epistemics. Some people more strongly value those who are similar to them in some sense, whether of the same nationality, gender, or even race. Even if the facts are agreed upon, they might still disagree on policy for this reason. As for epistemics, there are differences in who people are willing to trust for information and how much time people spend thinking about the world, which can drive differences in opinion when it comes to questions like "Does the COVID-19 vaccine work?" or "Do the Democrats want to give healthcare to undocumented immigrants?" This quiz is meant to identify your position on these two dimensions in short order.

Nationalism

Q1: Suppose you can give $1,000 to a random American. What amount of money do I need to offer to be distributed to a random foreigner for you to choose the foreigner instead? Assume they'll be just as well-off as the typical American, and that a proportionate amount of productive capacity is created in the world (for this thought experiment, by magic) when the money is distributed. Note If you're confused, think of this question as a way to identify how much more you value the happiness of other Americans over that of people in other countries, if at all, then re-read it. A proportionate amount of productive capacity is created so that you don't think of this as redistribution, and so that even someone who doesn't value foreigners at all would have a reason to input a number.

Q2: You can give $1,000 to a random American. What amount do I need to offer to be given to a random resident of a poor country (such as Somalia or India) for you to choose them instead? Like before, a proportionate amount of productive capacity is created.
